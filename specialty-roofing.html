Why A Builder

Specialty roofs are construction, not sales

Slate needs a framing check. Tile needs the right underlayment and battens. Metal needs engineered snow retention and the right clips. Coatings need a moisture survey. A company run by a commercial superintendent treats each one as the project it is, with a written scope, a schedule, and photos of everything.

  • Walk-on inspection and written scope. Materials by manufacturer and model, underlayment, flashings, ventilation, and snow retention.
  • Structural and code review. Weight, wind, snow load, wildfire zoning, and HOA guidelines checked before we quote.
  • Documentation for your insurer. Class 4 and Class A listings so you can claim the discount.
  • Honest recommendation. If the material you want is wrong for your roof or your exposure, we tell you and show you the alternative.

FAQ

Specialty roofing questions

Which specialty roof is best for hail?

Class 4 synthetic slate and shake, stone-coated steel, and Class 4 metal panels are the strongest hail performers. Natural slate and tile can crack under large hail. We show you the ratings side by side.

Which roof satisfies Colorado's wildfire code?

Any Class A covering: metal, tile, slate, and Class A synthetic assemblies. Natural wood shakes are restricted or prohibited in designated WUI zones and in several mountain jurisdictions.

Do specialty roofs qualify for insurance discounts?

Many do. Carriers commonly discount UL 2218 Class 4 roof coverings, which include many metal, stone-coated steel, and synthetic products. We provide the documentation for your agent.

Can my house carry slate or tile?

That requires a structural check. Slate and tile weigh several times what asphalt does. Where the framing cannot carry it, synthetic slate and tile deliver the look at a fraction of the weight.

The Questions

What a cause-of-loss inspection answers

Most hail disputes come down to three questions: is there hail damage, does it affect how the roof performs, and did it happen on the claimed date of loss? On the Front Range, where many roofs have been through more than one hailstorm, the third question is often the hardest.

Our inspection documents the physical evidence on every slope and ties it to the weather record, so each conclusion can be checked by the other side.

  • Presence. Hail-caused fractures, bruises, and granule displacement, separated from look-alike conditions.
  • Function. Whether damage reduces water shedding or service life, versus marks that change appearance only. Policy language controls coverage; we document the condition.
  • Timing. Whether the damage is consistent with the reported storm or with an earlier or later event.
  • Extent. Slope-by-slope counts that support, or do not support, a repair or replacement scope.
Hail vs. Wear

Hail damage and its look-alikes

Aging, blistering, foot traffic, and installation problems can resemble hail at a glance. These are the distinctions we document.

Roof componentConsistent with hailConsistent with wear or other causes
Asphalt shinglesRandom impact marks with displaced granules, a fractured mat or soft bruise under the mark, concentration on slopes facing the stormBlisters and pops, thermal cracking, uniform granule loss from age and UV, scuffs from foot traffic, manufacturing defects
Soft metalsRounded dents and spatter marks on vents, flashings, and gutters, sized and oriented consistently with the stormLadder and tool dents, dents on faces the storm could not reach, oxidized marks predating the storm
Wood shakesFresh splits with sharp, unweathered edges and an impact mark at the splitGrayed, weathered splits and checking along the grain from wet and dry cycles
Tile, slate, and metalFresh fractures with clean edges, dents matching hail size and directionCracks with dirt or growth in the break, walking breakage, fastener and installation damage
Methodology

How we inspect a hail loss

Test squares and counts

Where it is safe, we lay out a 10 by 10 foot test square on each directional slope, count hail-caused damage, and photograph each square with a scale. Counts are reported slope by slope.

Collateral damage

Soft metals, condenser fins, window screens, siding, fences, and painted surfaces show hail size and direction, and help separate one storm from another.

Weather records

NOAA's Storm Events Database, National Weather Service local storm reports, CoCoRaHS observer reports, and radar-based hail swath reports. Radar estimates hail aloft, so we treat it as corroboration, not proof.

Date-of-loss indicators

Weathering and oxidation at fractures and spatter marks, prior inspection photos, historical aerial imagery, permit records, and prior claims when they are produced.

Samples and custody

When samples are needed, removal is coordinated with the parties, photographed before and after, labeled, sealed, and logged on a chain-of-custody record.

Repairability

Whether a repair can meet the manufacturer's instructions and local code, including brittleness of aged shingles and Class 4 requirements in Fort Collins and unincorporated Larimer County.

Colorado Context

Why timing drives Colorado hail disputes

The Front Range from Fort Collins through the Denver metro sees frequent damaging hail, and many roofs carry marks from several storms. Disputes often hinge on a policy change, a prior paid claim, or a roof replaced between storms.

High-altitude UV and freeze-thaw cycles age roofing faster than in many regions, which is why separating wear from hail matters as much as finding the hail. We work across Larimer, Weld, and Boulder counties, the Denver metro, and the mountain communities, where storm reports are sparser and the physical evidence carries more weight.

FAQ

Hail cause-of-loss questions

Can you tell which storm caused the damage?

Often we can narrow it. We compare the physical condition of the marks, such as weathering, oxidation, and the freshness of fractures, with recorded storm dates, reported hail sizes, and prior photos. Where the evidence cannot separate two storms, the report says so.

What is the difference between functional and cosmetic hail damage?

Functional damage reduces a roof's ability to shed water or shortens its service life, such as fractured shingle mats or punctured metal. Cosmetic damage changes appearance only, such as shallow dents in metal panels. Policy language controls coverage; we document the condition so the parties can apply it.

Is radar hail data enough to prove a date of loss?

No. Radar estimates hail in the storm, not what reached a specific roof. We use NOAA Storm Events data, National Weather Service reports, and hail swath reports as corroboration alongside the physical evidence on the property.

Do you use test squares?

Yes. Where it is safe, we lay out a 10 by 10 foot test square on each directional slope, count hail-caused damage, and photograph each square with a scale. Counts are reported slope by slope.

Do you remove samples from the roof?

When needed and agreed by the parties. We photograph before and after removal, label and seal each sample, and keep a chain-of-custody log.

Who retains you for hail inspections?

Plaintiff and defense counsel, insurance carriers, and appraisers or umpires. We do not handle claims for policyholders or act as a public adjuster. Homeowners with recent storm damage should start with our storm damage page.

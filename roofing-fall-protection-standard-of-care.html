What We Review

Was the fall foreseeable and preventable?

Roofing falls are rarely a single failure. They usually involve a plan that did not match the roof, equipment that was missing or rigged wrong, or supervision that did not enforce what the safety program required.

We reconstruct what was planned, what was provided, and what was used, and compare it to the OSHA construction standards and to how competent roofing contractors in Colorado actually run a roof.

  • Planning. Site-specific safety and fall protection plans, and whether they fit the roof's height, slope, and width.
  • Equipment. Anchors, lifelines, harnesses, guardrails, warning lines, hole covers, and ladders: what was provided and how it was rigged.
  • Training and supervision. Fall protection training under 1926.503 and competent person oversight.
  • Site control. Who controlled the site and the means and methods among owner, general contractor, roofer, and subcontractors.
  • Conditions. Frost, snow, wet membranes, wind, skylights, and access points.
OSHA 1926 Subpart M

The fall protection rules in plain terms

A general summary of the federal construction standards. Colorado private-sector construction is enforced by federal OSHA rather than a state plan.

Six-foot trigger

In construction, fall protection is generally required when a worker is exposed to a fall of 6 feet or more to a lower level, under 29 CFR 1926.501(b)(1).

Low-slope roofs

On roofs of 4 in 12 or less, 1926.501(b)(10) allows guardrails, safety nets, personal fall arrest, or a warning line system combined with one of those or a safety monitor. A safety monitor alone is limited to roofs 50 feet or less wide.

Steep roofs

On roofs steeper than 4 in 12, 1926.501(b)(11) requires guardrails with toeboards, safety nets, or personal fall arrest systems.

Residential construction

Under 1926.501(b)(13), conventional fall protection is required unless the employer shows it is infeasible or creates a greater hazard, in which case a written, site-specific plan meeting 1926.502(k) is required. OSHA directive STD 03-11-002 governs enforcement.

Holes and skylights

Workers must be protected from falling through holes, including skylights, by covers, guardrails, or personal fall arrest under 1926.501(b)(4).

Systems and training

1926.502 sets system criteria such as anchorage strength and warning line placement, 1926.503 requires training by a competent person, and ladders fall under Subpart X.

Methodology

How we investigate a roofing fall

Scene documentation

Eave and ridge heights, slope by pitch gauge, roof width, anchor locations, access points, and the surface condition at the fall location, photographed and measured.

Equipment and custody

Harnesses, lanyards, lifelines, anchors, and ladders are inspected, labels and serial numbers recorded, and items held under a chain-of-custody log when the parties ask.

Records review

Safety programs, fall protection plans, job hazard analyses, toolbox talk and training records, daily logs, subcontracts allocating safety duties, and any OSHA inspection file produced.

Standard of care reaches beyond the regulation text to manufacturer instructions for fall protection equipment, consensus standards such as ANSI/ASSP Z359, and common practice on Colorado residential and commercial roofs. Where responsibility among several employers is at issue, we explain how OSHA's multi-employer citation policy describes creating, exposing, correcting, and controlling employers. FAQ

Roofing fall protection questions

When is fall protection required on a roof?

Under OSHA's construction standards, generally whenever a worker is exposed to a fall of 6 feet or more to a lower level. The acceptable systems depend on whether the roof is low-slope or steep and whether the work is residential construction.

Is a safety monitor enough on a residential reroof?

Usually not by itself. A safety monitoring system alone is an option for low-slope roofs 50 feet or less wide. On residential work, OSHA expects guardrails, safety nets, or personal fall arrest unless the employer shows those are infeasible or create a greater hazard and follows a written site-specific plan under 1926.502(k).

Does an OSHA citation prove negligence?

Not by itself, and the absence of a citation does not prove the work was safe. How OSHA regulations bear on the standard of care is a legal question for counsel and the court. We explain what the regulations require, what industry practice is, and what the evidence shows was done.

What evidence matters most after a roofing fall?

The equipment itself, photographs of the roof edge and anchors as they were, the fall protection plan, training records, and witness accounts of how the crew was tied off. Preserve the harness, lanyard, and anchor before anything is reused or discarded.

Can you evaluate the general contractor's role as well as the roofer's?

Yes. We review contracts, site control, the safety program, and supervision to show how responsibility was set up and carried out on the site, including how OSHA's multi-employer citation policy describes each employer's role.

Do you work on matters related to workers' compensation?

Yes, as technical support in related matters such as third-party liability and subrogation, where the jobsite safety facts are at issue. We provide technical opinions, not legal advice.

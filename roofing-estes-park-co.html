Permits and code in Estes Park and Larimer County

Reroofs inside the Town of Estes Park are permitted through the town's Building Division. Addresses outside town limits fall under Larimer County, which requires a Class A roof covering countywide, Class 4 impact-rated asphalt shingles on new and replacement roofs, an ice barrier from the eaves to at least two feet inside the exterior wall, and a signed roofing certification. Under Colorado's Wildfire Resiliency Code, enforced locally since July 2026, Class A coverings are required in designated wildland-urban interface zones and can be triggered when a large share of an existing roof covering is replaced. We confirm your jurisdiction and zoning before quoting.

Local Conditions

What Estes Park roofs are up against

Wind

Estes Park winds regularly exceed 80 miles per hour in winter. We install mechanically seamed standing seam, high-wind shingle systems, and sealed ridge details rated for it.

Snow and ice

Heavy snow and freeze-thaw cycles build ice dams on every eave. Full-coverage ice and water shield, balanced ventilation, and engineered snow retention are standard here.

Wildfire

The East Troublesome and Cameron Peak fires reached the edges of town in 2020. Class A metal, tile, and synthetic roofs with ember-resistant vents are the responsible choice and the code.

FAQ

Estes Park roofing questions

What roof is best for Estes Park?

Standing seam metal is the most common recommendation: Class A, sheds snow, handles wind, and lasts decades. Class A synthetic shake and stone-coated steel are strong options where an HOA or the look calls for a shingle or shake profile.

Does Larimer County require Class A and Class 4 roofs?

Yes. Larimer County requires a Class A roof covering throughout the county and Class 4 impact-rated asphalt shingles on new and replacement roofs, with an ice barrier and a roofing certification form on every job.

Can I still have a wood shake roof in Estes Park?

In designated wildfire zones, no, and we would not recommend it anywhere in the Estes Valley. Class A synthetic shake gives you the look without the fire risk or the insurance problem.

Do you install snow guards and heat cable?

Yes. Snow retention is laid out on a drawing for every mountain roof we build. Heat cable is used where the roof geometry leaves no better option, and we tell you when ventilation and insulation will solve the problem instead.

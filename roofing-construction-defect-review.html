What We Review

Where roof installations go wrong

Most roof defects live in the details: the laps, fasteners, and flashings that disappear under the finished roof. We open, measure, and photograph those details so the record shows what was actually installed.

We review steep-slope shingle, tile, slate, cedar, synthetic, and metal roofs, and low-slope TPO, EPDM, and modified bitumen systems on single-family, multifamily, HOA, and commercial projects.

  • Underlayment and ice barrier. Type, laps, and extent at eaves, valleys, walls, and penetrations.
  • Fastening. Nail type, count, placement, and depth against the manufacturer's pattern.
  • Flashing. Step, counter, headwall, chimney, skylight, kick-out, and drip edge details.
  • Ventilation. Intake and exhaust balance and its effect on ice dams, condensation, and warranties.
  • Low-slope membranes. Seams, terminations, drainage, crickets, and ponding.
Allocation

Workmanship, product, or design?

Allocation often decides a defect case. Each source leaves a different pattern.

Workmanship

Installation that departs from the manufacturer's instructions or the adopted code: high nails, short laps, missing kick-out flashing, unsealed penetrations. It usually repeats wherever the same crew did the same detail the same way.

Product

Failures that appear across the roof regardless of technique, such as widespread blistering, sealant strip failure, or factory seam defects. We record lot numbers, packaging, and warranty registration where available.

Design

Details that cannot perform as drawn: inadequate slope on low-slope areas, missing crickets behind wide chimneys, valleys discharging against walls. Questions involving structure or building science beyond roofing are flagged for the right professional.

Methodology

How we investigate a roof defect

Document review

Plans, specifications, submittals, permits and inspection records, warranty registrations, daily logs, and the manufacturer's installation instructions in effect when the roof was installed.

Non-destructive survey

Every slope and detail photographed and mapped, with moisture readings and controlled water testing where they help locate an entry point.

Destructive testing

Test cuts and tear-backs under a protocol agreed by the parties. Each layer is photographed and measured, samples are preserved under chain of custody, and openings are left weathertight.

Code analysis

Findings tied to the IRC Chapter 9 or IBC Chapter 15 edition and local amendments the jurisdiction had adopted when the work was permitted, not today's code.

Repair scope

A repair that fixes the defect without overreaching, priced line by line. See our cost estimate services.

Rebuttal

Review of opposing defect reports and repair scopes, item by item.

Colorado Context

Local code adoption and CDARA

Building codes in Colorado are adopted locally. Cities and counties adopt editions of the International Residential Code and International Building Code with their own amendments, so the governing requirements in Fort Collins, Greeley, Boulder, Denver, or a mountain town can differ.

The Construction Defect Action Reform Act

Colorado's Construction Defect Action Reform Act (CDARA, C.R.S. 13-20-801 and following) sets a notice-of-claim process that generally requires a claimant to give a construction professional written notice before filing suit, with an opportunity to inspect and to offer to repair or settle. Limitation and repose periods for construction claims appear in C.R.S. 13-80-104.

Our role is technical: attending and documenting notice-of-claim inspections, evaluating proposed repairs, and preparing defect and repair-cost opinions. Deadlines, damages, and procedure are for counsel. We provide technical opinions, not legal advice.

FAQ

Construction defect questions

How do you tell a workmanship defect from a product defect?

By pattern and by evidence under the surface. Workmanship defects usually follow the installer, repeating at the same details. Product defects tend to appear across the roof regardless of technique. We open representative areas, measure what was installed, and record product lot information where available.

Which code edition applies to the roof?

The edition and local amendments the jurisdiction had adopted when the work was permitted, together with the manufacturer's installation instructions in effect at the time. The report identifies both.

Can you attend a CDARA notice-of-claim inspection?

Yes. We can attend for either side, document conditions and any destructive testing, and evaluate a proposed repair. We provide technical opinions, not legal advice on CDARA deadlines or procedure.

Do you perform destructive testing?

When the parties agree on a protocol. We photograph each layer, measure and record what we find, preserve samples under a chain-of-custody log, and leave openings weathertight.

Do you review multifamily and commercial roofs?

Yes. We review single-family, multifamily, HOA, and commercial buildings, including steep-slope shingle, tile, slate, metal, and low-slope membrane roofs.

Can you price the repair?

Yes. We prepare line-item repair scopes and cost estimates tied to the defects we identify, including access, staging, and code-required work.

Evaluating a roof defect claim?

Send the parties and the project. We follow up the same or next business day.

Why It Works Here

Cedar is beautiful. The rules around it have changed.

Colorado's statewide Wildfire Resiliency Code requires Class A roof coverings in designated wildland-urban interface areas, and several mountain jurisdictions, including Aspen, Summit County, and Boulder, already prohibit natural wood shakes. Many insurers surcharge or decline wood roofs. We still install cedar where it is permitted and we will tell you plainly when it is not, and what to put up instead.

The honest tradeoffs

Natural cedar in Colorado's dry, high-UV climate typically lasts 20 to 30 years with maintenance, less than it does in the Pacific Northwest. It is combustible, the fire-retardant treatment degrades over time, and insurers know it. In WUI zones the code may not allow it at all, and replacing more than a quarter of an existing roof can trigger Class A requirements for the whole covering. If you love the look, synthetic shake gives you 50-year warranties, Class A and Class 4 ratings, and no insurance fight. If you have an existing cedar roof in good shape, we will help you keep it as long as it makes sense.

FAQ

Cedar Shake Roofing questions

Can I still get a cedar shake roof in Colorado?

In many Front Range neighborhoods, yes, with fire-retardant-treated shakes. In designated wildland-urban interface zones and in jurisdictions like Aspen, Summit County, and Boulder, natural wood roofs are prohibited or restricted. We check your address before we quote.

Will my insurance cover a wood shake roof?

Some carriers surcharge wood roofs, some write them with cosmetic or age limits, and some decline them outright. Ask your agent before you commit. A Class A synthetic shake usually resolves the problem.

How long does a cedar roof last here?

Plan on 20 to 30 years for a well-installed, treated, maintained cedar roof in Colorado. Sun and dryness split and cup the wood faster than in wetter climates.

What replaces cedar and still looks like it?

Polymer synthetic shakes from manufacturers such as DaVinci, Brava, and CeDUR, and stone-coated steel shake profiles. All are Class A, most are Class 4, and they are lighter than real cedar.

Can you repair my cedar roof instead of replacing it?

Often, yes. Individual shakes, flashings, and ridge can be repaired. If your jurisdiction's code triggers full replacement at a certain percentage, we tell you where that line is before any work starts.

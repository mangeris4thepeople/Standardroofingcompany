What We Answer

The roofing questions behind the case

Roofing disputes rarely turn on whether a roof leaks. They turn on why it failed, when, who was responsible, and what a proper repair costs. We answer those questions from field evidence, the building code adopted where the work was done, and the manufacturer's installation instructions.

Engagements cover residential, multifamily, HOA, and commercial buildings with asphalt shingle, tile, slate, cedar, synthetic, metal, and low-slope membrane roofs. We can be retained as a consulting expert, a testifying expert, or both.

  • Cause of loss. Hail, wind, age, installation error, or a product problem, and whether the damage matches the claimed date of loss.
  • Construction defect. Whether the work followed the adopted code and manufacturer requirements, and whether a failure traces to workmanship, product, or design.
  • Standard of care. Whether a roofing contractor, general contractor, or site supervisor met code, OSHA 1926 Subpart M, and accepted industry practice.
  • Cost and scope. What a code-compliant repair or replacement includes and what it reasonably costs in the local market.
  • Rebuttal. Line-by-line review of opposing expert reports, carrier scopes, and contractor estimates.

Methodology

How we build an opinion

Documentation first

Every slope, elevation, and detail is photographed with a scale and indexed to a roof diagram. Measurements, pitch, and layer counts are recorded in the field, not estimated from a desk.

Standards, not hunches

Findings are tied to the IRC Chapter 9 or IBC Chapter 15 edition and local amendments in force when the work was permitted, the manufacturer's installation instructions, and published industry references.

History and weather

Permit records, prior claims and inspections, historical imagery, NOAA Storm Events data, National Weather Service storm reports, and hail swath reports where timing matters.

Samples and custody

When samples or equipment are removed, removal is coordinated with all parties, photographed before and after, labeled, sealed, and logged on a chain-of-custody record.

Limits stated plainly

Our opinions are roofing and construction opinions, not engineering reports. If a question calls for a licensed professional engineer, we say so at scoping.

Plain language

Reports are written so a judge, jury, mediator, or appraisal panel can follow the reasoning from photograph to conclusion.

FAQ

Roofing expert witness questions

Can you serve as a consulting expert, a testifying expert, or both?

Yes. Many engagements start as consulting so counsel can evaluate the issues before deciding whether to disclose us. The role is set in the written retention agreement and can change as the case develops.

What do you need to run a conflict check?

The names of the parties, the property address, any insurers or contractors involved, and your firm. Do not send privileged material through the intake form. We check conflicts before we review anything substantive.

Do you provide a CV, fee schedule, and prior testimony disclosures?

Yes. A CV and fee schedule are provided on request, along with any prior testimony disclosures the rules require, and our reports are prepared to support Fed. R. Civ. P. 26 and C.R.C.P. 26 expert disclosures.

Are your reports engineering reports?

No. Our opinions are roofing and construction opinions based on field inspection, the adopted building code, manufacturer installation instructions, and industry practice. When a question requires a licensed professional engineer, such as structural capacity, we say so at scoping so counsel can retain one.

Where in Colorado do you work?

Colorado statewide, with focus on the Front Range from Fort Collins and Greeley through Boulder and the Denver metro, plus the mountain communities. Travel is addressed in the retention agreement.

How quickly can you inspect and report?

It depends on access, weather, and the size of the record. At the scoping call we confirm whether we can meet your inspection and disclosure deadlines before we accept the engagement.

Do you give legal advice or coverage opinions?

No. We provide technical opinions about roofs and construction, not legal advice. Coverage, liability, and damages are questions for counsel and the trier of fact.

Retaining a roofing expert?

Send the parties for a conflict check. We follow up the same or next business day.
